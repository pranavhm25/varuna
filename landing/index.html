The problem

A district mean cannot show you a rain shadow.

The hobli is the revenue circle used for disaster declarations, relief funds and crop insurance payouts, and it holds between 10 and 30 villages. State and district forecasts still smooth it into a single number, so the dry pocket and the wet ridge arrive as one comfortable average.

Varuna starts from the station record instead: 4,250 cleaned station seasons across 31 districts, every departure recomputed, and every static baseline normal flagged.

What you get

One workspace, four habits of evidence.

Hobli resolution

850 stations per season, so a deficit pocket is not averaged into a comfortable district figure.

Leakage free features

Every input is computed from years before the target season, so the hold out test stays honest.

Geography held out

Five fold district grouped validation keeps whole districts out of training, not just random rows.

Caveats in the open

Suspect normals are flagged wherever they appear and are never silently corrected.

A district average is comfortable. Eight hundred and fifty hobli readings are honest.

Read this before the accuracy figure

A model that misses 94% of 2025 drought events cannot serve as an early warning system on its own.

The majority class baseline scores 79.65% accuracy by guessing stable everywhere and catching no drought at all. The workspace shows the classifier verdict next to the evidence that limits it, so a stable label is read as a weak signal.

Questions

Answered plainly.

What exactly is a hobli?

A hobli is a revenue circle below the taluk, usually covering 10 to 30 villages. It is the tier used for disaster declarations, relief allocation and crop insurance payouts in Karnataka.

Is Varuna an official drought declaration?

No. It is an academic machine learning project. The risk zones follow project thresholds: deficit at −20% or below, excess at +40% or above. A district officer still owns the declaration.

Where does the rainfall data come from?

Karnataka State Natural Disaster Monitoring Centre annual tables, archived by the OpenCity Karnataka portal. Five seasons are published: 2020, 2021, 2022, 2024 and 2025, under the Open Data Commons Attribution licence.

Why is 2023 missing?

KSNDMC published no hobli level table for calendar year 2023. Varuna does not impute it. The gap is carried as an explicit feature, gap_years = 2, and the charts mark the missing season.

How accurate is the classifier?

On the 2025 held out season it reaches 70.82% accuracy with 0.4077 macro F1 and 6.06% drought recall. Five fold district grouped validation gives 30.49% drought recall. A majority class guess scores 79.65% accuracy and finds no drought at all, which is why accuracy is not the headline.

Why is there no map?

The source tables contain no latitude, longitude or boundary geometry. No coordinates were invented, so districts are compared as a departure matrix and as the terrain on this page, not as a map.

What is a suspect normal?

A station whose reported normal never changes across seasons while its departures stay one sided. 338 stations match that pattern. They are flagged everywhere they appear and are never silently corrected.

Can I reuse the data or the code?

Yes, with attribution. The data is released under the Open Data Commons Attribution licence and the project source is public on GitHub. Cite Varuna if you reuse the analysis.
